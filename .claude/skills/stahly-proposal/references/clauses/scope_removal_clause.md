# Asymmetric scope-removal clause (T&M NTE proposals)

**When to use:** Any proposal where one or more phases are billed time-and-materials with a not-to-exceed cap, and the client has expressed cost sensitivity to specific feature-code items. The clause gives the client a real lever to reduce actuals without re-papering the contract, while protecting Stahly from scope-creep additions that would erode margin.

**Strategic intent:** Asymmetric. The client can subtract without amendment; additions require amendment. This:
- Keeps the NTE cap intact (we hold our hours estimate against the worst case)
- Gives the client a transparent mid-execution removal mechanism (they see the labor concentrate, they direct removals, savings flow to them in actuals)
- Protects Stahly from "remove item X then add items Y and Z silently" pattern that drains margin

**Where it lives in the proposal:** In the "Changes in the Identified Scope of Services" section, typically as paragraph 3 (between the general additional-scope T&M paragraph and any project-specific carve-outs).

**Sample language (Hilger-Roy, 2026-06-01):**

> Within the Classification phase (billed time-and-materials, not to exceed $24,000), Fergus Electric or HDR Engineering may direct Stahly to remove or reduce specific feature-code items from the deliverable at any time during execution, without a contract amendment. Time already spent on a removed item prior to the removal direction will be billed at standard rates; only un-completed work on that item is dropped going forward. The not-to-exceed cap is unaffected by removals. Additions to the classification scope, or any increase to the not-to-exceed cap, require a written amendment to this agreement.

**Reusable template (substitute bracketed values):**

> Within the [PHASE NAME] phase (billed time-and-materials, not to exceed $[NTE_AMOUNT]), [CLIENT NAME] [or DESIGN ENGINEER] may direct Stahly to remove or reduce specific [FEATURE-CODE / DELIVERABLE / SCOPE] items from the deliverable at any time during execution, without a contract amendment. Time already spent on a removed item prior to the removal direction will be billed at standard rates; only un-completed work on that item is dropped going forward. The not-to-exceed cap is unaffected by removals. Additions to the [PHASE NAME] scope, or any increase to the not-to-exceed cap, require a written amendment to this agreement.

**The four guardrails that matter (don't drop any of these):**

1. *"without a contract amendment"* — names the right granted (removal without paperwork)
2. *"Time already spent... will be billed at standard rates; only un-completed work... is dropped going forward"* — prevents the client from removing scope after we've done the work and refusing to pay
3. *"The not-to-exceed cap is unaffected by removals"* — prevents the client from removing scope then arguing for a lower cap retroactively
4. *"Additions... require a written amendment"* — makes the clause asymmetric (subtractions free, additions paid)

**When NOT to use:**
- Pure firm-fixed proposals — the removal mechanism doesn't apply
- T&M without NTE — there's no cap to protect, so the asymmetry doesn't matter
- Proposals where the client has explicitly agreed to a fixed scope and we want to lock against changes

**Historical note:** First used on the Fergus Electric Hilger-to-Roy LiDAR proposal (June 2026) after HDR (Andy Fiske) suggested mid-discussion that conductor attachment classification might not be needed. Rather than negotiating feature-by-feature on the front end (and giving up margin we might need), the clause was added to let HDR decide at execution time when they could see what was actually being captured. Strategic upside: kept us covered if the full scope held; gave them a real reduction lever without renegotiation.
